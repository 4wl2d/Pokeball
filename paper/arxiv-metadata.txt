Title: Ballarch: Boundaries in Time and Honest Outcomes, from Register-Transfer Logic to Services

Authors: Vladislav Tomilov
# [AUTHOR DETAILS PENDING] Confirm the name exactly as on the arXiv account,
# and the affiliation "Independent researcher" in the paper's author block.

Abstract:
Software architecture styles draw boundaries in space: they constrain which code may depend on which. They say little about when an effect may leave a component, or what it may claim after a timeout, when retrying can charge a customer twice and reporting failure can hide a charge that happened. We present Ballarch, which adds two boundaries in time: the commit horizon, the atomic commit of the decision that requests an effect, and the send horizon, the first moment the request may have left the runtime. Transactions, transports and durable execution use these instants locally; Ballarch makes them boundaries of every interaction. Each piece of state has one owner that decides in pure functions. A runtime commits state and requested effects atomically, and ends every outbound request in exactly one outcome, Done, NotDone or Unknown, chosen from its phase and its port's effect class. For operations made of several requests, a knowledge algebra over Kleene's strong three-valued logic bounds what a component may claim. It is sound, and exact for independent calls; a composite that replies with settled knowledge is an honest component one level up. The rules are stated once. Level-specific mechanisms check the call-protocol rules in a Kotlin runtime (trace oracles, an Alloy model), C firmware (bounded power-loss exploration) and a SystemVerilog block (netlist checks and a fault-injecting bench).
