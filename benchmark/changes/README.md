# Change requests

Coupling and cohesion metrics are proxies for one question: how much code does a change touch? This suite answers it directly for the benchmark's checkout. Four change requests (CRs) are specified below in prose, and each is implemented as a patch against the scenario-A code of every design. A script measures each patch, and a second script checks that every patched design still meets every correctness requirement, old and new, under the benchmark's faults and crashes.

The specifications below were written and committed before any design was changed. Three things were changed afterwards, each said where it applies: before any design was changed, CR1's client and CR4's second requirement; after the first runs, two requirements (MissedFailover in CR3 and UnchargedFailure in CR4) that correct designs violated at crash points where the policy itself makes the order fail. The verification's run counts were raised after the first run, from 500 runs per cell and 100 sweep seeds to the numbers given under Method, and the formatting rule under Method was written while the patches were. The sections from [How each design made the change](#how-each-design-made-the-change) on were written after the measurements.

After the first measurement, a reviewer showed that the formatting rule then stated (a line stays on one line up to 165 characters, "the longest line in the designs' existing code") gave a wrong reason, since the longest existing line has 168 characters, that it was not applied the same way in every patch, and that one comparison depended on it: at 168 characters the careful MVU version's CR3 patch shrank from 30 to 20 physical lines, and Ballarch's 23 became the largest instead of falling within the careful versions' range. In response, three things were changed after the measurements. The rule was corrected and is now checked by `measure.py --check`; four patches changed only their line breaks (layered and hardened CR1, careful actors and careful MVU CR3), and the careful MVU CR3 patch also went back to the `if … else` expression of the existing code, which it had turned into a braced block; no patch changed what it does. The headline count became logical lines, which do not depend on line wrapping, with physical lines and tokens given beside it; units touched are now found from the logical lines too, which no longer charges a unit for an added or removed closing brace that `difflib` had aligned with that unit's own (one unit fewer in four patches of the other designs, none of Ballarch's; no range changed). And import lines are now also added to the code lines, since Ballarch's patches change more of them than the others. The CR2 policy now names one difference from the charge's policy that the first text left implicit; the policy itself is unchanged. The first version of these results is in the repository's history.

## What every change request shares

- **The base scenario does not change.** Stock, orders, fault profiles, crash model, seeds and the seven requirements of the [benchmark](../README.md#requirements-the-oracles) apply as before. A CR adds client requests, external services, requirements (oracles) and, for CR1, a terminal state.
- **New external services behave like the payment provider in scenario A.** Every request to them independently gets one of the same fates (answered in time, answered after 5 s, lost before arrival, processed with the answer lost, processed then connection reset, connection refused, and under Beyond horizon delivered after the horizon), drawn from the same random source in the order the requests are sent. Each declares the same 1 s delivery horizon. None honours idempotency keys.
- **The designs keep their policy.** A design that reconciles (the four careful versions and Ballarch) applies the policy written in each CR; it is the policy of the base scenario carried over to the new effect, with the one difference stated in CR2. A naive version applies the naive policy: one request, and a timeout or I/O error means the effect did not happen.
- **What counts as the change.** A patch changes only the scenario-A feature code of one design (`benchmark/<design>/src/main/kotlin/<root package>`, without its scenario-B packages). The shared simulation (`env`), the toolkits (`kit` packages) and Ballarch's kernel and runtime are not changed by any patch; everything a CR needs from the environment is added to `env` once, for every design, before the patches are written. A patch must not reformat, rename or restructure code that the CR does not need changed. The scenario-B variants call the scenario-A constructors and use its types, so several of them no longer compile with a patch; the edits they would need are not part of the patches and not counted (see [Method](#method)).

## CR1: cancel a completed order within a window, and refund it

**Business rule.** A customer may cancel an order within 30 s of the time the system accepted it. A cancellation of an order that is `Completed` when the system receives it, and that arrives within the window, is accepted: the system refunds the order's charge through the payment provider, then returns the order's unit to stock, then records the order as `Refunded`, a new terminal state. Any other cancellation (the order is not completed, or the window has passed) is rejected and changes nothing. While an accepted cancellation is being processed, the order has no terminal state.

**Client.** The customer of `o1` looks at the order every 100 ms until it has a terminal state, and cancels it as soon as they see it `Completed`; they stop looking at 30 s. The customer of `o2` cancels at 40 s, whatever its state; `o3` is never cancelled. Every order is accepted at 0 or, after a crash at 0, at 50 ms, so the cancellation of `o1` is within the window and that of `o2` is not. The client is at least once: right after a restart it sends again every cancellation it sent before the restart, so the system must treat a repeated cancellation as one. (A first draft had `o1` cancel at a fixed 10 s. Since a crash time is drawn from the busy period, most crashes would then have fallen in the idle gap before the cancellation and few during the refund; the cancellation was tied to the completion before any design was changed. The client's checks are scheduler tasks, so they add crash points to the sweep.)

**Refund API.** `Provider.refund(orderId, amount)` refunds the order's charge. The provider never refunds more than it charged for an order: a refund request for an order with nothing left to refund is answered `NothingToRefund` and has no effect. Repeating a refund request is therefore safe, as with refunds that name the charge they refund at real providers.

**Policy of the designs that reconcile.** Send up to three refund requests. A request that gets no answer within 1 s, or fails (connection reset or refused), is sent again at once. The first answer, `Refunded` or `NothingToRefund`, means the charge is refunded. After three unanswered requests the order goes to `NeedsAttention`. After a restart, an accepted cancellation that is not finished is resumed and its refund sent again; whether the budget of three starts again after a restart is left to each design.

**Naive policy.** One refund request; no answer within 1 s or an error means the refund failed: the cancellation is dropped and the order stays `Completed`. After a restart, an accepted cancellation that is not finished is resumed and its refund sent again.

**New requirements.**

| Violation | Definition |
|---|---|
| CancelIgnored | the cancellation of `o1` was sent while `o1` was `Completed`, and `o1` did not end `Refunded` or `NeedsAttention` |
| RefundWithoutCancel | an order without a cancellation inside the window (`o2`, `o3`) was refunded or reported `Refunded` |
| FalseRefund | an order reported `Refunded` was not refunded |
| UnreportedRefund | an order was refunded but ended neither `Refunded` nor `NeedsAttention` |
| RefundStockLeak | an order reported `Refunded` still holds stock |

## CR2: award loyalty points once per completed order

**Business rule.** Every completed order earns 10 loyalty points, awarded through a loyalty service after the order has been recorded `Completed`. A failed order, or one that needs attention, earns none. The order's outcome stays `Completed` while points are awarded; the award has a state of its own, reported per order as `Awarded`, `NeedsAttention` (handed to an operator) or `Failed` (the system gave up without telling anyone).

**Loyalty API.** `LoyaltyService.award(orderId, points)` has no idempotency keys: every award request that reaches the service awards the points again. `LoyaltyService.status(orderId)` is Safe and reports whether points were awarded for the order. Both have the fault model above and a 1 s delivery horizon.

**Policy of the designs that reconcile.** The charge's write-ahead record, budgets and reconciliation applied to the award, with one difference: a status read after the horizon that says the order was not charged fails the order in the base scenario, while one that says the points were not awarded leads to another award request if the budget allows, because a completed order must earn its points. Beyond the horizon, that request lets a late first award take effect after the status said it had not, so the reconciling designs award some orders twice there (DoubleAward under Beyond horizon in the results). This paragraph's first sentence was added after the measurements; the bullet points below are the policy as first written and implemented.

- record durably that an award may be in flight before sending it;
- send an award again only after evidence that it did not take effect: connection refused, or a status read after the horizon that says it was not awarded; at most three award requests per order, counted durably;
- when the outcome is unknown (no answer within 1 s, or a reset), wait past the horizon, then ask the status, at most three queries; "awarded" ends the award, "not awarded" sends it again if the budget allows;
- when the budget of requests or queries is spent, hand the award to an operator (`NeedsAttention`);
- after a restart, reconcile an award that may have been sent; never send it again without that evidence.

**Naive policy.** One award request; no answer within 1 s or an error means the points were not awarded, and the award is recorded `Failed`. After a restart, an award that was being sent is sent again.

**New requirements.**

| Violation | Definition |
|---|---|
| DoubleAward | the loyalty service awarded points for an order more than once |
| AwardWithoutCompletion | points were awarded for an order that did not end `Completed` |
| FalseAward | an award reported `Awarded` did not take effect |
| MissingAward | an order ended `Completed`, and its award is neither `Awarded` nor `NeedsAttention` |

## CR3: a second payment provider with failover

**Business rule.** A secondary payment provider with the same API, faults and delivery horizon as the primary, and like it without idempotency keys, is added. A checkout charges through the primary as before. When the primary provably did not charge the order, the checkout charges the order through the secondary instead of failing it. The order fails only when the secondary provably did not charge it either. A declined charge is not retried at the other provider.

**Policy of the designs that reconcile.** The base policy at each provider, with the same budgets (three sends, three status queries). The primary provably did not charge an order when three requests were refused, or when its status, read after the horizon, says it did not. When a provider's outcome stays unknown, the order goes to `NeedsAttention`; it is never charged at the other provider. After a restart, a charge that may have been sent is reconciled at the provider it was sent to.

**Naive policy.** No answer within 1 s or an error at the primary means the payment failed there, so the order is charged through the secondary, with one request; no answer or an error there fails the order. After a restart, the charge of an order that was being paid is sent again to the provider it was being paid through.

**New requirements.** The seven base requirements count the charges at both providers: DoubleCharge holds when an order was charged twice in total, at one provider or at both. In addition:

| Violation | Definition |
|---|---|
| MissedFailover | an order the primary was asked to charge ended `Failed`, and the secondary was never asked to charge it or for its status |

The first version of MissedFailover counted only charge requests at the secondary. In a first run with 500 runs per cell and 100 sweep seeds, the careful actors version then violated it at 40 of 12,658 swept crash points: a crash between recording that the order moves to the secondary and sending the request there, after which the order's possible charge is reconciled at the secondary, which says it was not charged, and the order fails, as the policy says. Status queries now count as trying the secondary.

## CR4: remove the inventory

**Business rule.** The shop now sells a digital product with unlimited stock. Stock reservation is removed: no order is reserved, released or failed for lack of stock. Every accepted order is charged, and an order fails only when its payment is not made. The payment policy of each design does not change.

This CR removes a component instead of adding one. It is in the suite because the [decision guide](../../docs/decision-guide.md) cites an earlier, unpublished measurement in which adding or removing a component touched more files in Ballarch's module structure than in single-module baselines.

**New requirements.** Oversell and StockLeak no longer apply. In addition:

| Violation | Definition |
|---|---|
| UnchargedFailure | an order ended `Failed` although the provider was never asked to charge it or for its status |
| ReservationKept | the system reports a stock reservation at the end of the run |

UnchargedFailure first counted only charge requests. In a first run with 100 sweep seeds, the careful actors and MVU versions then violated it at 1,565 of 13,247 and 506 of 8,394 swept crash points, where the crash came after the charge was recorded as possibly sent and before the request left: after the restart the charge is reconciled, the provider says it was not charged, and the order fails, as the policy says. As for CR3, a status query now counts.

## Method

- **Patches.** `<cr>/<design>.patch` is a unified diff, relative to the repository root, against the scenario-A code of the design as it is in this checkout. Each applies on its own (`git apply --check`). One formatting rule applies to every patch: no line it adds is longer than 168 characters, the longest line in the nine designs' existing code (`benchmark/saga-careful/.../keyed/payment/PaymentService.kt`, line 54; the longest in their scenario-A code has 163), and a statement that was on one line and that the patch changes stays on one line if it fits within 168 characters. New code is formatted like the parallel existing code: for example, a new `runCatching { … }.fold(…)` in an actors version is wrapped as the existing one is, although it would fit on one line. `measure.py --check` checks the first two parts of the rule (a wrapped logical line counts as a changed one-line statement when the block it replaces holds a one-line statement with a `difflib` similarity ratio of at least 0.6 to it) and that 168 is still the longest existing line; the third is a judgement. Only the physical line count depends on the rule.
- **Measurement.** [`measure.py`](measure.py) reads each patch and the files it changes, and reports the files touched (changed, new or deleted), the top-level units touched (a top-level class, interface or object with everything nested in it; the top-level functions and properties of a file count as one unit, the class the compiler generates for them), and three sizes, each added, removed and changed:
  - *code lines*, the headline: logical lines, that is statements, declaration headers and `when` branches, however they are wrapped. A physical line is joined to the logical line before it while a `(` or `[` is open, when it starts with `.`, `?.`, `?:`, `&&`, `||`, `)`, `]`, `as` or an `else` that is not a `when` branch, when the line before ends with a binary operator, `=`, `,`, `:`, an infix call such as `to`, or a `->` that does not end a lambda's parameters, when it holds only closing brackets, when it is the `get()` of the property declared on the line before, and when the line before holds only annotations. Logical lines are compared as token sequences, so wrapping, indentation, comments and a trailing comma do not count. A braced block still counts its statements and its `} else {` lines, so writing an `if … else` as a block instead of an expression is a difference in code, not in formatting;
  - *physical lines*: lines that hold code, compared without indentation, the convention of RESULTS.md section 9; they depend on the formatting rule above;
  - *tokens* (identifiers, keywords, literals, operators and brackets; a string literal is one token) added and removed, which depend on no formatting but count a qualified name such as `CheckoutState.Refunding` as three tokens and an explicit type argument list such as `<CheckoutState, Unit>` as five.

  All three are compared per file by `difflib`, with `package` and `import` lines left out; in a replaced block, the first min(old, new) lines count as changed and the rest as added or removed. The import lines added or removed are counted separately, and RESULTS.md also gives code lines with them, because they are part of what a reviewer reads and Ballarch's patches change more of them. The script writes the patch metrics and each patch's `git apply --numstat` to [`results/changes.json`](../results/changes.json); `measure.py --check` fails if a patch no longer applies, if its `git apply --numstat` or its numbers differ from the recorded ones, or if it breaks the formatting rule.
- **Verification.** [`verify.py`](verify.py) copies `reference/kotlin` and `benchmark` to a temporary directory and applies every patch of a CR. It then compiles each patched design with its scenario-B packages and records which of their files no longer compile: the scenario-B variants call the scenario-A constructors (Ballarch's `CheckoutFlow`, the layered `CheckoutInteractor`, …) and use its message types, so a patch that changes them breaks the variant. Those edits are not in the patches, and RESULTS.md section 13 lists the files per CR and design. It then removes the scenario-B packages, builds with Gradle and runs [`runner`](runner) (`:benchmark:changes`), which runs each design under the CR's scenario: 2,000 runs per profile with and without a crash, as in the benchmark, and the exhaustive single-crash sweep on 500 seeds under Stress and Mild. It checks the base requirements and the CR's own. It runs the unpatched designs the same way, so that the table shows that the new requirements catch a design without the change. Only the designs and this runner are built with the patches: the benchmark runner, which uses the scenario-B packages, and the tests are not. The results go to `results/changes.json` with the patch metrics, with the run counts recorded per CR.
- **Who wrote the patches.** The same author as the designs, after the designs' results were known; see [Threats](#threats-to-validity).

## Threats to validity

- One author wrote every design and every patch, knowing the results of the base benchmark. Each patch is one reading of how an expert in the style would make the change; another expert could make a smaller or a larger one.
- Four CRs on a feature of 96–279 SLOC ([RESULTS.md](../results/RESULTS.md) section 8) are a small sample. Three of them add or change interaction with an unreliable external service, the problem the benchmark and Ballarch are about, which is where Ballarch's runtime is meant to help; one removes a component. A CR that changes only business rules or data is not in the suite.
- The fault policy of each CR was written by the author. Where it is stricter than what a team would do (for example, three requests and then an operator), every reconciling design applies the same one.
- The size of a change depends on what is counted. Between the reconciling designs, the differences in CR3 and CR4 are a few lines, and Ballarch's rank changes with the measure: in CR2 its change is the smallest of the five by code lines and physical lines, and the fourth smallest by tokens and with import lines. Physical lines also depend on the formatting rule, which was corrected after a reviewer showed that one ranking flipped with it (see the top of this page).
- The patches leave out the scenario-B variants' call sites (see Method), and only the designs and the change requests' runner are built with them: the benchmark runner and the tests are not.

## How each design made the change

Each patch follows the design's own structure; none restructures code the change request does not need changed. In brief:

| Change request | Layered (naive and careful) | Actors (naive and careful) | MVU (naive and careful) | Outbox + saga (naive and careful) | Ballarch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CR1 refund | The order gains its acceptance time and two statuses; the use case gains `cancel` and a refund step, and receives a clock; the payment gateway port and adapter gain `refund`. | The checkout actor gains a `Cancel` command, refund stages and the acceptance time; the payment actor gains `Refund`; the guardian keeps the checkout actors' references, so that it can deliver a cancellation. | `Place` and `Cancel` carry the time; a `Refund` command, refund stages; `init` resumes a refund under way. | The saga gains `cancel` and refund steps; the payment participant handles a refund command. | The checkout Ball takes a second request, so its request type becomes a sealed interface; the acceptance time is carried through three states; two new states. The payment Ball takes `Refund` (its request type also becomes a sealed interface), with two states and a refund port declared `Idempotent`, bound to a new adapter. |
| CR2 loyalty | The order gains an award status and a durable count; a loyalty gateway port and adapter; the use case runs the award after completion (careful: with the charge's write-ahead and reconciliation). | A loyalty actor; the checkout actor continues after completion with the award (careful: write-ahead stages, a timer, a status query). | Award fields, messages and commands; `init` reconciles an award that may have been sent (careful). | A loyalty participant on its own channel; the saga gains an awarding step (careful: the saga counts requests and checks; the participant writes an award-attempted row). | The checkout Ball publishes `OrderCompleted`; a new loyalty Ball subscribes to it and reconciles the award as the payment Ball reconciles a charge (sending it again after "not awarded", as CR2 prescribes), through two new ports and adapters; the composition root binds them. |
| CR3 failover | The order records its provider; the use case gets a second gateway and charges through it when the first failed (careful: when the first provably did not charge). | A second payment actor; the checkout actor records its provider. | The model records the provider; commands name it. | A second payment participant on its own channel; the saga records the provider (careful: a declined charge becomes its own reply, so that it is not retried at the other provider). | The payment Ball gains two ports for the secondary provider and records which one a charge went to; the composition root binds two more adapters. |
| CR4 no inventory | The inventory port, its adapter and the reservation status are deleted. | The inventory actor, its messages and the reservation stages are deleted. | The stock count is deleted from the model. | The inventory participant, its messages and the compensation step are deleted. | The inventory Ball, its port and messages, and the checkout's reservation and release states are deleted. |

## Results

Measured as described above; full tables in [RESULTS.md section 13](../results/RESULTS.md#13-change-impact-four-change-requests), raw data in [`results/changes.json`](../results/changes.json).

- **Correctness.** With each change, the four careful versions and Ballarch violated no requirement, old or new, in any Stress or Mild cell (8,000 runs per design and change request) or at any of the 25,305–96,455 swept crash points per design and change request. Without the change, every design violated the change request's own requirements. Beyond the horizon, the reconciling designs awarded points twice in 170–187 of 4,000 runs per design in CR2 (the second award request after a "not awarded" status, the one difference from the charge's policy) and charged twice in 150–151 runs in CR3 (the primary charging late, after the failover); in CR3 the failover also cut FalseFailure from 153 runs to 5. The naive versions kept their violations.
- **Code lines touched** (logical lines added, removed and changed), Ballarch against the four careful versions: CR1 73 against 34–43; CR2 50 against 64–68; CR3 18 against 11–16; CR4 38 against 8–48; 179 in total against 122–173. With the import lines added or removed: CR1 87 against 36–45; CR2 84 against 71–89; CR3 20 against 11–16; CR4 51 against 8–53; 242 against 131–192. Physical lines: 97, 73, 23 and 58, 251 in total, against 38–54, 80–94, 12–20 and 10–59, 155–220. Tokens: 1,140, 899, 249 and 720, 3,008 in total, against 341–553, 633–974, 104–179 and 109–584, 1,527–1,954.
- **Files touched**: Ballarch 2–4 per change request, the careful versions 2–5. **Units touched**: Ballarch 16, 9, 5 and 9, the careful versions 5–6, 6–8, 3–6 and 3–7.
- **Scenario-B call sites left out**: with the patches, 0–2 files of each design's scenario-B variants no longer compile (none for the MVU variants); see RESULTS.md section 13.

CR1 was the costliest change for Ballarch: two Balls had to accept a second kind of request, and the acceptance time had to be carried through the checkout's states. Ballarch's CR3 change was also the largest of the five reconciling designs by every measure, and so was its total. CR2, a second effect without idempotency keys, was the one where it needed the fewest code lines and physical lines of the five reconciling designs, but not the fewest tokens or code lines with imports (the fourth smallest of five on both). Every reconciling design, Ballarch included, wrote the award's reconciliation again; what Ballarch did not write is the write-ahead record, the classification of each attempt and the recovery after a restart, which its runtime provides. In CR4 its change fell within the careful versions' range by lines and was the largest by tokens.
